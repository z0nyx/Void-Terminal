# Acceptable Use Policy

**Last updated: September 6, 2026**

> Void has no public content, comments, chat, or social features — it is
> a single-user terminal client. This document is titled "Acceptable Use
> Policy" rather than "Community Guidelines" for that reason: there is
> no community inside the App to moderate. What follows instead is the
> set of conduct rules that govern how the App itself — and the systems
> you use it to connect to — may be used.

## 1. Purpose

This Acceptable Use Policy sets out what you may not do with Void ("the
App"). It applies to anyone using the App, and violating it is a
violation of the [Terms of Service](./terms.md).

## 2. Prohibited Uses

You may not use the App to:

1. **Spam** — send unsolicited bulk messages or automate abusive
   traffic through any connection made with the App.
2. **Commit fraud** — impersonate a person, organization, or system, or
   use the App as part of any deceptive scheme.
3. **Gain unauthorized access ("hacking")** — connect to, probe, or
   attempt to access any server, account, or system you are not
   expressly authorized to access. The App's SSH client functionality
   must only be used against hosts you own or have explicit permission
   to connect to.
4. **Distribute malware** — use the App's local shell or SSH session to
   create, store, transmit, or deploy viruses, worms, ransomware, or any
   other malicious software.
5. **Handle illegal content** — use the App to store, transmit, or
   process content that is illegal under applicable law.
6. **Infringe intellectual property** — use the App to copy, transmit,
   or distribute content in violation of copyright, trademark, or other
   intellectual-property rights (see the
   [Copyright/DMCA Policy](./copyright-policy.md) for how to report
   this).
7. **Circumvent App restrictions** — reverse-engineer, patch, or
   redistribute a modified build of the App in a way that misrepresents
   it as the official release, or that bypasses security controls (such
   as host-key verification) in a way intended to deceive a user of
   that modified build. This does not restrict your rights under the
   App's MIT source-code license (see [`terms.md`](./terms.md) §3 and
   [`legal-notice.md`](./legal-notice.md)) to legitimately fork, modify,
   and redistribute the open-source code under its own terms.

## 3. Your Responsibility for Remote Systems

Void is a general-purpose terminal client. It has no way to know whether
you are authorized to access any given host, and it does not attempt to
police that on your behalf beyond the safeguards described in the
[Privacy Policy](./privacy.md) (host-key verification). Responsibility
for ensuring you have authorization to access any system you connect to
rests entirely with you, and unauthorized access to computer systems is
independently illegal in most jurisdictions regardless of what tool is
used to carry it out.

## 4. Enforcement

Void has no accounts to suspend or ban (see the
[Account Deletion Policy](./account-deletion.md) — there is nothing to
enforce against on the Developer's side). Enforcement of this policy, in
practice, means:

- The Developer may decline to assist with, or may publicly close, a
  GitHub Issue or contribution that clearly violates this policy.
- Distribution platforms (Google Play, RuStore) enforce their own
  policies independently and may remove the App from their store if it
  is used in a way, or found to contain functionality, that violates
  their respective developer policies.

## 5. Reporting a Violation

If you become aware of the App being used, or a public fork/redistribution
of it being presented, in a way that violates this policy, report it via
GitHub Issues:

**https://github.com/z0nyx/Void-Terminal/issues**
